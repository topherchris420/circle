  1. Evaluationt = 240.0 s

    Arousal index 8.76 from heart rate z 11.4, skin conductance z 7.9 and SCR rate z 4.4. Signal quality passed: beat coverage 95%, no motion. Window ends 1 s before the decision.

    Source ranges: EDA #12,735–#15,294, PPG #19,830–#23,815, IMU #79,686–#95,702

  2. DecisionPBR-1

    Second consecutive evaluation at or above the trigger index of 2.0, so the controller starts paced breathing: one haptic cue every 10 s (6 breaths/min).

    MODEL_RESULT record, MODEL_INFERRED, CRC-32C sealed

  3. Actuation7 cues

    Each GO command is followed by a TLV3201 current edge on GPIO46 after a median 0.28 ms, and by vibration that the IMU observes after a median 2.57 ms. Against hidden truth the physical onset error stays within 2.92 ms, about one 2.5 ms IMU sample.

    EVENT records: command, electrical onset, completion, physical observation

  4. ReleasePBR-1-STOP

    At t = 310.0 s the index read 0.08, below the release level of 1.0 on two consecutive evaluations after the 60 s minimum.

    MODEL_RESULT record with decision_id

    All 59 evaluations re-derive bit-for-bit from the raw bundle alone. The INTERVENTION record lists every command, electrical onset and physical observation by evidence id.

Both arms share every random draw, so they are identical until the first cue. The difference is produced by the twin's assumed response to paced breathing. It demonstrates that the evidence chain can resolve an effect; it is not evidence that the effect exists in people.

What this does not show

All data are simulated by a phenomenological twin; no person or hardware was involved. Agreement with the twin tests the software's measurement, timing, and evidence logic, not physiology, calibration, or clinical accuracy. SpO₂ uses an uncalibrated curve and reads about 1 point low by design. The arousal index is an engineering trigger, not a psychological measure. CIRCLE Rev B remains engineering-review-only hardware.
