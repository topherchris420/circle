# CIRCLE Rev A Architecture and Schematic Design

**Status:** Approved design basis for an engineering-review schematic

**Date:** 2026-08-14

**Target:** Bench-first experimental research hardware

**Release class:** Engineering review only; not approved for fabrication or human use

## 1. Purpose

CIRCLE Rev A is an open experimental instrument that closes a physical feedback loop around the Vers3Dynamics software ecosystem. It acquires synchronized physiological time-series data, preserves raw measurements and timing evidence, exchanges data and decisions with VitalSync and Dynamic Resonance Rooting (DRR), applies adaptive feedback, and exposes the instrumentation needed for Embedded AI Validation Platform (EAIV) verification.

CIRCLE is experimental research hardware. It is not a medical device and must not be represented as diagnosing, treating, preventing, or monitoring a medical condition. Completion of this engineering-review schematic does not establish electrical safety, biocompatibility, electromagnetic compatibility, measurement accuracy, or suitability for human-subject research.

The operating loop is:

`Human -> CIRCLE -> VitalSync -> DRR -> adaptive decision -> CIRCLE feedback -> Human -> measurement`

The design principle is:

`Sense -> Synchronize -> Model -> Intervene -> Measure -> Compare -> Adapt`

## 2. Scope and Deliverable

Rev A consists of two printed-circuit assemblies:

1. **CIRCLE mainboard:** compute, timing, IMU, EDA acquisition and hardware interlocks, removable storage, USB-C, battery power path, isolated SYNC IN/OUT, haptic drive, expansion, and validation hooks.
2. **CIRCLE PPG contact board:** MAX30102-class optical sensor, local power conditioning and logic translation, identity/calibration memory, keyed cable interface, mechanical light-exclusion provisions, and unpopulated support for a contact-point accelerometer.

The implementation deliverable is a combined architecture and schematic package:

- editable system and safety-boundary diagrams with rendered SVG exports;
- a KiCad hierarchical schematic project for the mainboard;
- a separate KiCad schematic project for the PPG contact board;
- preliminary component selections and review BOM;
- pin-allocation, interface, timing, power-budget, safety-analysis, and validation documents;
- ERC and project-open evidence generated by a supported `kicad-cli` installation.

The package is explicitly an **engineering-review draft**. Safety-critical values, power sizing, isolation layout, footprints, and connector mechanics remain release gates. They must be marked with structured review fields rather than hidden comments or implied confidence.

## 3. Goals

Rev A shall:

- record raw red/IR PPG, raw EDA/front-end measurements, and raw six-axis IMU data without a host;
- use device-monotonic time as the sole authoritative session timeline;
- retain sequence, timing, status, provenance, fault, and dropped-data evidence;
- continue deterministic acquisition through characterized microSD write stalls;
- capture and generate isolated laboratory trigger events with measured latency and jitter;
- command haptic feedback and distinguish command, electrical actuation, and measured physical response;
- physically disable active EDA measurement when USB, a non-isolated debug adapter, or an exposed non-isolated expansion connection is present;
- expose sufficient instrumentation for EAIV timing, storage, power, interlock, fault-injection, and signal-quality testing;
- remain serviceable enough that an optical-head change does not force redesign of compute, storage, or power architecture.

## 4. Non-Goals

Rev A does not include:

- a medical-device claim or certification claim;
- isolated USB;
- a wearable-size optimization;
- closed-loop treatment or autonomous clinical decision-making;
- an audio or cymatic output implementation;
- a production enclosure;
- a fabrication release based only on schematic review;
- a guarantee that firmware can detect every possible physical breakdown of an isolation barrier.

## 5. System Partition and Trust Boundaries

### 5.1 Electrical domains

The mainboard contains two deliberately separated domains:

- **BAT/HUMAN domain:** battery, charger system node, ESP32-S3, sensors, microSD, haptic drive, EDA front end, and the human-connected electrode interface.
- **LAB_ISO domain:** external sides of SYNC IN and SYNC OUT, referenced only to the BNC shields and powered through reinforced isolation.

The PCB shall maintain an explicit no-copper isolation barrier. No plane, guard trace, mounting feature, test point, shield tab, ESD return, or silkscreen-associated copper may cross it. Creepage and clearance shall be calculated from the selected isolator certification, working voltage category, pollution degree, board material, coating decision, and applicable laboratory-equipment requirements. The schematic must visually identify both domains and use distinct ground symbols.

USB-C is part of the BAT/HUMAN domain and is not isolated. USB attachment therefore forces EDA inactive.

### 5.2 Fail-off EDA enable chain

EDA activation uses a two-stage hardware sequence. Preparation is permitted only when all primary safety conditions are true:

`EDA_PREPARE = FW_REQUEST & BATTERY_VALID & !USB_PRESENT & !DEBUG_ATTACHED & !EXTERNAL_EXPANSION_ATTACHED & SAFETY_POWER_GOOD`

`EDA_PREPARE` powers and initializes the disconnected EDA analog section. Electrode connection is permitted only after its rail is valid:

`EDA_ACTIVE = EDA_PREPARE & EDA_ANALOG_GOOD`

The equations are implemented with hardware logic, window supervisors, and fail-safe output polarity. Firmware supplies `FW_REQUEST` but cannot bypass another term. Every asynchronous unsafe input shall dominate the request, de-energize both normally-open electrode switches, and remove power from the EDA excitation/front-end path.

The electrical meanings are:

- `BATTERY_VALID`: asserted by a hardware window monitor only when a connected cell is between 3.20 V and 4.25 V and the battery-temperature input is within the selected cell manufacturer's permitted operating window. Voltage hysteresis shall be at least 100 mV. Battery absent and charger-only operation never assert this term.
- `SAFETY_POWER_GOOD`: asserted only after the dedicated safety-logic rail remains between 3.15 V and 3.45 V for at least 10 ms. It shall deassert asynchronously within 10 microseconds when the rail falls below 3.00 V, exceeds 3.55 V, or the supervisor loses power. An active-low/open-drain topology shall make broken or unpowered wiring disable the path.
- `EDA_ANALOG_GOOD`: asserted only after the selected EDA analog rail is inside its component-qualified window and stable for at least 10 ms. It shall deassert asynchronously on undervoltage, overvoltage, regulator fault, or monitor power loss.

Exact supervisor parts may move these thresholds inward but may not widen the permitted windows. USB removal during charger-only operation results in loss of `SAFETY_POWER_GOOD` before electrode switches can remain energized. A partially powered EDA analog section cannot assert `EDA_ANALOG_GOOD`.

The safe state is:

- both electrode conductors physically open through normally-open components;
- excitation source unpowered;
- EDA ADC/front-end power removed or held in a defined inactive state;
- firmware-visible reason inputs asserted when the processor is powered;
- loss of firmware, reset, brownout, or broken enable wiring incapable of energizing the path.

Two separately packaged, normally-open switching elements shall interrupt the two electrode conductors. In the normal disabled state, both conductors are open. Under a single switch-weld fault, at least the opposite conductor must remain open; the design does not claim bilateral opening after that fault. Passive current-limiting networks remain in series with both conductors even when switching components fail closed. Under every analyzed single fault, the acceptance requirement is either at least one open electrode conductor or residual current below the documented fault-current limit. Final values shall be selected from a documented worst-case current and leakage analysis that includes tolerance, maximum battery/system voltage, charger states, ESD leakage, ADC protection networks, op-amp input structures, unpowered rails, reversed connectors, and one-at-a-time component faults.

### 5.3 Detectable and non-detectable isolation faults

Rev A shall monitor:

- isolated-side power-good state;
- digital channel activity through a controlled loopback test;
- assembly configuration and isolation-option population;
- external connector attachment states that can be sensed without bridging the barrier.

Those checks establish functional health only. Dielectric puncture, contamination, conductive debris, incorrect creepage, and some assembly faults require visual inspection, continuity/isolation resistance testing, and hipot testing. Firmware must not label functional loopback success as proof of galvanic isolation.

## 6. Compute and Timing Architecture

### 6.1 Compute module

The provisional compute module is **ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8**, providing 16 MB external flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM, Wi-Fi, BLE, and native USB. The module choice remains provisional until the GPIO allocation, ambient-temperature requirement, antenna placement, sourcing, and PSRAM operating-mode review pass.

The implementation shall reserve boot-strapping pins, native USB pins, flash/PSRAM-connected pins, and antenna keepout before assigning peripherals. No external connector may directly expose a boot-strapping pin without protection that preserves deterministic boot behavior.

### 6.2 Authoritative time

A single 64-bit monotonic ESP32 hardware-derived counter is authoritative for the session. It shall not step because of network time, host wall clock, USB attachment, or model inference. The firmware shall convert raw counter values to a documented device-time unit without reducing ordering precision.

Each acquisition and event source also retains native timing evidence:

- interrupt or data-ready capture counter;
- sensor FIFO level and overflow state;
- sensor sequence number;
- sensor-native timestamp or sample counter when available;
- configured nominal sample period;
- mapping/calibration record from native source time to device time;
- timestamp uncertainty or quality status when reconstruction is required.

PPG FIFO batches shall not receive one shared timestamp. Individual sample times shall be reconstructed from sequence and configured period, anchored to captured interrupt timing, while retaining uncertainty and FIFO evidence. IMU-native timestamps and external-clock state shall be retained rather than discarded. EDA conversions shall be anchored to captured DRDY edges.

Host-clock mapping consists of timestamp pairs and mapping metadata. Host time never replaces recorded device-monotonic timestamps.

### 6.3 Acquisition targets

The nominal balanced profile is:

| Stream | Nominal rate | Design-throughput rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| PPG red | 100 samples/s | 200 samples/s |
| PPG IR | 100 samples/s | 200 samples/s |
| EDA raw | 32 samples/s | 64 samples/s |
| Main accelerometer | 200 samples/s per axis | 400 samples/s per axis |
| Main gyroscope | 200 samples/s per axis | 400 samples/s per axis |
| Contact accelerometer, if populated | 100 samples/s per axis | 200 samples/s per axis |

Rates are configurable only within characterized operating profiles. A configuration change is a timestamped event and cannot silently alter the interpretation of an existing stream.

## 7. Sensor Architecture

### 7.1 PPG contact board

The provisional optical sensor is **MAX30102**. It shall expose raw red and raw IR FIFO samples. Heart rate, pulse estimates, signal-quality metrics, and other features are derived records and must link back to their source sample ranges.

The daughterboard shall include:

- MAX30102 and manufacturer-recommended local decoupling;
- local 1.8 V regulation for sensor logic;
- local I2C level translation between the 3.3 V cable and 1.8 V device;
- separately filtered LED supply;
- local identity/calibration EEPROM with unique board identity and schema version;
- interrupt routing;
- series-damping and filtering footprints selected after cable characterization;
- pads and connector allocation for a low-power contact-point accelerometer, initially not populated;
- optical/mechanical keepouts for a light-excluding contact fixture.

The cable shall be short, keyed, locking, and replaceable. A JST-GH-class connector is the design basis. Logic power, LED power, and ground returns shall be assigned to control pulse-current return paths. Cable length, pull-ups, edge rate, capacitance, ESD behavior, connector insertion, and powered/unpowered backfeed shall be measured before the optional contact accelerometer is populated by default.

The contact accelerometer becomes default-populated only if the test evidence shows that its motion-artifact information materially improves PPG interpretation without compromising I2C margin, power, or contact mechanics.

### 7.2 Main IMU

The provisional IMU is **ICM-42688-P**, connected through dedicated SPI with dedicated interrupt/data-ready lines. The design shall expose its optional clock input and permit a stable external reference to be fitted after clock-tree review. FIFO contents, overflow flags, device timestamp information, raw acceleration, and raw angular-rate data shall be retained before fusion or classification.

### 7.3 EDA front end

The EDA method is low-voltage DC conductance measurement intended to preserve tonic and phasic information. The provisional ADC is **ADS1220**, connected over a dedicated SPI interface with a dedicated DRDY input.

The schematic shall separate these functions:

1. fail-off electrode disconnection;
2. passive bidirectional current limiting and input protection;
3. low-noise excitation generation;
4. differential sensing and bias control;
5. calibration injection and electrode-disconnect detection;
6. ADC conversion and digital isolation from noisy loads through layout/power partitioning, not galvanic separation inside the battery domain.

The ADS1220 current sources may be used for calibration or diagnostics only after fault analysis. They are not the sole human-current limiter. Raw ADC codes, gain/reference configuration, calibration identity, saturation, settling, electrode-disconnect state, and protection status shall be recorded.

The engineering-review schematic must contain the current-limit equations and named analysis variables. Numerical component values become fabrication-eligible only after worst-case safety review and bench verification with a human-body/electrode simulator.

## 8. Power Architecture

The provisional power chain is:

`USB-C 5 V -> input protection/VBUS detect -> BQ24074 charger and power path -> system/battery node -> partitioned rails`

Partitioned rails are:

- 3.3 V digital rail from a TPS63070-class buck-boost converter;
- filtered low-noise EDA analog rail;
- local PPG 1.8 V logic rail;
- switched/filterable PPG LED rail;
- separately switched microSD rail;
- separately switched haptic rail;
- separately switched isolated-SYNC input power;
- protected switched expansion power.

USB-C is a 5 V sink with explicit CC resistors and USB 2.0 ESD protection. VBUS presence is detected ahead of charger behavior so charging, enumeration state, suspended USB, or firmware state cannot hide attachment from the EDA interlock.

The battery connector shall support a protected single-cell LiPo with temperature sensing. Charger current and thermal limits shall be selected from the actual cell rating, connector rating, copper area, enclosure assumptions, and measured worst-case concurrent load.

Kelvin shunt or jumper-selectable current-measurement footprints shall cover battery/system input and the major digital, analog, optical, storage, haptic, and isolated-interface branches. Measurement hooks shall not create an unlabeled external-ground path while EDA is enabled.

## 9. USB, Debug, and Expansion Interlocks

USB VBUS directly disables EDA in hardware. Firmware separately logs `EDA_DISABLED_USB_ATTACHED` with device timestamp, sequence, prior state, and debounce/status evidence.

The non-isolated programming/debug connector shall include a mate-detect contact that directly disables EDA. The connector shall be keyed and shrouded or use a controlled cable system. Silkscreen and documentation shall state that attaching an arbitrary grounded probe to BAT/HUMAN ground also invalidates EDA operation even if the connector detect mechanism is bypassed.

Expansion is divided into:

- **internal same-domain expansion**, intended for enclosed battery-domain daughterboards; and
- **externally accessible expansion**, whose attachment detect forces EDA inactive unless a future reviewed isolated interface is populated.

The internal connector may expose protected power, ground, I2C, SPI, device-domain timing/event signals, and reserved GPIO. It shall not expose boot-strapping or critical acquisition pins without isolation buffers or configuration resistors that preserve boot and acquisition behavior.

## 10. Isolated SYNC IN/OUT

The provisional isolation component is **ISOW7742**, providing two channels in each direction plus isolated power. The schematic shall use the reinforced-isolation variant and package appropriate to the calculated creepage/clearance target. One channel in each direction carries SYNC IN and SYNC OUT. The remaining forward channel drives a controlled health challenge, and the remaining reverse channel returns isolated-side power-good/health status through simple LAB_ISO-domain logic.

On the LAB_ISO side:

- SYNC IN accepts protected 3.3 V and 5 V TTL/LVTTL pulses through a resistor network, negative clamp, ESD protection, Schmitt comparator, and defined hysteresis;
- SYNC OUT drives an N-channel MOSFET as open drain, allowing external 3.3 V or 5 V pull-up without a manual voltage selector;
- BNC shields connect only to `LAB_ISO_GND`;
- external-side protection returns only to `LAB_ISO_GND`;
- internal and external test points are physically and visually distinguished.

Each detected or generated event records device timestamp, sequence, direction, edge/polarity, status, and calibration identifier. The timestamp represents the internal captured/generated event. Calibrated input and output propagation delays, pulse-width distortion, edge slew, and jitter are separate metadata.

A controlled loopback fixture shall exercise both functional directions. The dedicated challenge/status pair shall verify isolated-side power and logic response without consuming the SYNC signals. Loopback and challenge response prove functional timing and power state, not dielectric integrity.

## 11. Storage Architecture

The primary recorder is removable microSD on a dedicated 4-bit SDMMC bus. The connector shall include card detect. Series-damping footprints, ESD protection, local bulk/high-frequency decoupling, and a separately controlled rail are required.

The buffering path is:

`sensor DMA/FIFO -> internal-SRAM DMA buffers -> PSRAM record rings -> record assembler -> asynchronous SD writer`

Internal SRAM is used at peripheral/DMA boundaries unless the selected ESP-IDF and silicon configuration are proven to support the intended external-memory DMA path. PSRAM provides long-stall buffering after bounded copies from acquisition buffers.

The initial requirement is usable buffering for at least 60 seconds at twice the balanced acquisition profile while retaining enough internal memory for deterministic operation. Final allocation shall be based on measured encoded data rate, task high-water marks, radio configuration, and characterized card stalls.

Storage behavior shall include:

- append-oriented framed binary records;
- chunked writes and periodic CRC-32C;
- preallocation where card/filesystem behavior supports it;
- missing-card, mount, write, removal, full-card, buffer-pressure, overflow, and incomplete-session events;
- exact source sequence/time ranges in gap records;
- a user-visible finalize/safe-eject command;
- crash recovery that scans frame lengths and CRCs and discards only the invalid tail;
- clean trailer emission only after queues and filesystem data have been flushed.

Onboard flash stores firmware, configuration, identity, calibration metadata, crash/fault records, and bounded recovery information. It is not a silent substitute for long-duration raw recording.

## 12. Haptic Feedback

The provisional haptic driver is **DRV2605L** with a connectorized LRA as the primary actuator and retained ERM compatibility. Haptic power and return paths shall be partitioned from EDA and optical acquisition, with local energy storage, filtering, and scheduled-operation support.

Four distinct evidence classes shall be recorded:

1. requested intervention and decision identifier;
2. command written to the haptic driver;
3. electrical actuation detected by a current-sense threshold/capture path;
4. physical vibration observed by the main IMU or a dedicated accelerometer.

Driver current does not prove physical motion. IMU observation does not prove the intended waveform without calibration. Completion and fault logic shall retain both electrical and physical evidence and shall not collapse them into one boolean.

## 13. Firmware Execution Model

Firmware shall use bounded tasks and interrupt paths for:

1. monotonic time and event capture;
2. PPG, EDA, and IMU acquisition;
3. hardware safety-state supervision and reason logging;
4. internal-SRAM transfer and PSRAM ring management;
5. record assembly;
6. asynchronous SD storage;
7. feedback actuation and evidence capture;
8. BLE/Wi-Fi telemetry;
9. USB debug/export;
10. health, watchdog, reset, and fault reporting;
11. optional model inference.

Acquisition, timestamp capture, and safety supervision outrank storage, networking, USB transfer, and inference. High-priority paths shall avoid filesystem calls, network calls, unbounded queues, heap allocation after session start, and logging formats with unbounded execution time.

Core affinity shall be selected from measured ESP-IDF behavior and radio coexistence tests rather than assumed from a generic dual-core template. Radios shall be independently disableable for low-noise acquisition.

## 14. Record and Provenance Model

Every frame shall contain:

- magic and schema version;
- record type;
- mandatory provenance class;
- header and payload lengths;
- device-monotonic time range;
- source sequence range where applicable;
- quality/status flags;
- CRC-32C.

Allowed provenance classes are:

- `RAW_MEASURED`
- `DERIVED`
- `MODEL_INFERRED`
- `SIMULATED`
- `TEST`
- `INTERVENTION`

There is no default, null, generic, or unknown provenance value. Schema validation rejects missing or unsupported provenance.

The classes apply by causal origin, not by record shape:

- `RAW_MEASURED` covers direct sensor samples, captured external edges, and direct hardware status observations before algorithmic interpretation;
- `DERIVED` covers deterministic transformations and firmware-assembled metadata such as session/configuration descriptors;
- `MODEL_INFERRED` covers probabilistic or learned-model outputs;
- `SIMULATED` covers generated data intended to imitate a physical source, even when it uses a normal sample payload format;
- `TEST` covers explicit fixture, calibration-injection, and fault-injection records whose primary purpose is validation;
- `INTERVENTION` covers decisions, commands, and generated outputs intended to change the measured system.

When more than one label could appear plausible, the class describing the record's immediate causal origin wins. A test fixture that injects simulated PPG data therefore produces `TEST` or `SIMULATED`, never `RAW_MEASURED`; the test manifest records the fixture relationship separately.

Record types include session header, stream descriptor, sample chunk, event, clock mapping, configuration change, gap, fault, model result, intervention, and clean trailer.

Derived and inferred records shall link to source stream IDs, exact source time/sequence ranges, algorithm or model identity/version, configuration hash, execution location, and output provenance. Simulated and test data may not enter a raw-measured stream. Intervention records link the adaptive decision to command, electrical actuation, physical observation, and subsequent comparison windows.

## 15. Hierarchical Schematic Structure

The mainboard project shall use these sheets:

1. `00_root` — system hierarchy, domain boundaries, connectors, and cross-sheet nets;
2. `01_compute_usb` — ESP32-S3, native USB, boot/reset, programming, identity straps, and LEDs;
3. `02_power` — USB-C sink, charger/power path, battery, regulators, rail switches, shunts, and sequencing;
4. `03_eda_safety` — electrode connector, disconnects, passive limits, excitation, protected AFE, ADC, calibration, and interlock logic;
5. `04_sensors` — main IMU and PPG cable interface;
6. `05_storage` — microSD, SDMMC conditioning, card detect, and storage power;
7. `06_sync_isolation` — reinforced isolation, LAB_ISO power, BNC input/output conditioning, and keepout notes;
8. `07_feedback_expansion` — haptic drive/evidence, internal expansion, external-attachment interlock, and protected GPIO;
9. `08_observability` — test points, current hooks, fault-injection headers, revision straps, and validation signals.

The PPG project shall use separate root, optical sensor/power, identity/interface, and optional-motion sheets when hierarchy improves clarity. If the daughterboard remains sufficiently small, those functions may be kept on one sheet with clearly bounded blocks; the electrical requirements do not change.

## 16. Four-Layer Layout Intent

- **L1:** components and short critical signals;
- **L2:** continuous BAT/HUMAN reference plane, interrupted only by the real isolation barrier;
- **L3:** partitioned power and secondary signals;
- **L4:** remaining signals and components.

The design shall physically separate EDA analog, optical cable/LED return, SDMMC, haptic switching, radio/antenna, and LAB_ISO circuitry. Plane splits inside one electrical domain shall not be used as a substitute for return-current planning. The ESP32 antenna keepout excludes copper, components, battery, cables, enclosure metal, and test fixtures.

Isolation barriers require explicit creepage/clearance dimensions and no-copper rules on every copper layer. Slotting is permitted only if the selected fabrication and assembly process can verify it.

## 17. Observability and Fault Injection

Labeled test points shall cover:

- every generated rail and power-good signal;
- reset, boot mode, and revision identity;
- sensor buses, interrupts, and data-ready lines;
- EDA analog nodes that can be exposed without defeating current limiting;
- each stage of the EDA safety chain;
- USB, debug, and expansion attachment detection;
- SD clock/command/data activity and card detect;
- internal and LAB_ISO sides of SYNC;
- haptic command, current threshold, and fault signals;
- device clock/capture outputs suitable for logic-analyzer correlation.

Fault-injection provisions shall permit controlled simulation of card removal, full buffers, stuck safety inputs, loss of isolated power, sensor interrupt loss, haptic open/short behavior through a protected fixture, and reset/brownout. Fault-injection headers must not create an accidental external-ground path during EDA-active testing.

Status LEDs shall have hardware or firmware disable control and default to off during optical or low-power validation profiles.

## 18. Validation Gates

### 18.1 CAD and electrical review

- Both KiCad projects open without parser repair.
- ERC runs with no unexplained errors; each intentional exception is documented at the exact net or pin.
- All symbols have manufacturer part numbers or an explicit engineering-review classification.
- All footprints are checked against the manufacturer package drawing before fabrication release.
- Pin allocation is checked against ESP32 strapping, USB, flash/PSRAM, antenna, SDMMC, and peripheral requirements.

### 18.2 Timing

- Device time has resolution of 1 microsecond or better, never moves backward, and has no unmarked discontinuity within a session.
- Captured EDA DRDY and IMU interrupt edges receive a device timestamp within 10 microseconds at the 99.9th percentile and 25 microseconds maximum under the design-throughput load.
- Post-mapping timestamp uncertainty is no greater than 100 microseconds for EDA and IMU and 1 millisecond for PPG. A sample outside its validated uncertainty bound is retained with degraded timing status rather than accepted silently.
- Every stream preserves exact sequence continuity or an explicit gap covering every missing sequence value.
- No acquisition queue exceeds 75% occupancy during a 24-hour design-throughput run with the selected radio profile.
- Each SYNC direction has no more than 2 microseconds calibrated propagation delay, 250 nanoseconds peak-to-peak jitter, and 500 nanoseconds pulse-width error for input pulses of at least 10 microseconds. Polarity and threshold behavior shall match the interface specification at both 3.3 V and 5 V.
- Haptic electrical onset occurs within 5 milliseconds of the local command timestamp, and measured physical response begins within 25 milliseconds for the selected actuator/profile. A missed limit produces a fault record rather than a successful intervention record.

### 18.3 Storage

- Test multiple representative cards.
- At the balanced profile, retain at least 120 seconds of measured buffer autonomy; at twice that profile, retain at least 60 seconds.
- Complete a 24-hour run at twice the balanced profile with zero unexplained sample loss, zero backward timestamps, and zero CRC-invalid committed chunks.
- Inject a continuous 45-second SD write stall at twice the balanced profile with zero sample loss and no acquisition queue above 90% occupancy.
- The worst observed stall for an accepted card/profile shall consume no more than 75% of measured buffer autonomy.
- Test missing, full, removed, corrupted, and slow cards.
- Test reset and brownout during header, payload, CRC, and trailer writes.
- Recover every CRC-valid complete frame before an interrupted or corrupt tail, never emit a false clean trailer, and report exact gaps after buffer exhaustion.

### 18.4 Safety

- Verify EDA opens on USB insertion in every battery, charger, reset, bootloader, crash, and firmware-hang state.
- Repeat for debug and externally accessible expansion attachment.
- Inject one-at-a-time stuck or failed safety components and confirm that at least one electrode conductor remains open or residual current remains below the documented fault-current limit.
- Measure electrode current and leakage under normal and credible single-fault conditions with tolerance and temperature margins.
- Verify ESD structures and unpowered devices do not create hidden current paths.
- Perform isolation resistance, continuity, and applicable hipot testing across the SYNC barrier.
- Confirm intended SYNC connections remain galvanically isolated and intended debug or external-expansion attachment forces EDA inactive before their grounds connect. Treat arbitrary probes, clips, modified cables, and fixtures that bypass mate-detect as procedural hazards outside the hardware guarantee; require EDA-disabled test procedures for them.

### 18.5 Signal quality

- PPG: ambient light, contact pressure, cable motion, LED current, temperature, skin-contact repeatability, and motion artifact.
- EDA: noise, settling, usable conductance range, disconnect, saturation, calibration, motion, and injected common/differential interference.
- IMU: bias, scale, clock stability, FIFO timing, and vibration coupling.
- Compare mainboard and contact-point acceleration if the optional daughterboard accelerometer is fitted.

### 18.6 Provenance and falsifiability

- Reject records with missing or unsupported provenance.
- Reject inferred values represented as raw measurements.
- Inject dropped samples, clock mapping changes, storage faults, simulated streams, and test data.
- Verify exported data preserves the injected distinctions and exact invalid intervals.
- Verify every adaptive decision and intervention can be traced to source records and subsequent comparison windows.

## 20. Completion Criteria

The engineering-review package is complete when:

- architecture and safety-boundary diagrams agree with the schematic hierarchy;
- both KiCad projects open and pass documented ERC review;
- every connector pin, rail, domain, and interlock signal is named and traceable;
- provisional component selections and release gates are explicit;
- the power budget and GPIO allocation close at the design-throughput profile;
- safety analysis covers normal operation and credible single faults without claiming certification;
- timing, storage, signal, interlock, and provenance validation procedures are executable;
- no unresolved fabrication-critical decision is disguised as an ordinary component value;
- the package is labeled engineering-review-only and not approved for human connection.
