First Light · physical-sensor demonstration

Real light through a real fingertip. Every decision kept.

A phone camera measures a pulse. Six quality gates decide whether that evidence is good enough to act on. A bounded sound follows the measurement. The sensor is disturbed on purpose, and the system must refuse. Then a second, independent program rebuilds every decision from the raw frames.

Not a medical device · Camera frames never leave this page

  1. MeasureCamera frames, 30 a second
  2. AssessSix gates on the last 8 seconds
  3. RespondA bounded sound follows the pulse
  4. DisturbThe sensor is failed on purpose
  5. RefuseSilence, with the reason on record
  6. VerifyEvery decision rebuilt from raw frames

Choose a source

Works where the browser can switch the camera light on (Chrome on Android does). Without the light, press your fingertip against a bright lamp. Rest your hand on a table. The gates were calibrated on two phone models; on yours they may refuse, and that refusal is a valid result. The session takes about a minute.

Open the phantom screen on a second device at full brightness. Hold this camera a hand's width away so the red field fills the view. Cover the lens when asked. Not yet run on physical devices by the authors: treat a pass or a failure as a first result.

What has been tested

What this does not show

  • A camera pulse rate is not a medical measurement. Accuracy here means within 5 bpm of ECG over 8 seconds.
  • The system is cautious by design. For some people and phones it never acts.
  • The sound is a bounded command. No effect on any person is tested or claimed.
  • Hashes detect change. They do not prove a live bundle came from a camera; only the public-dataset sessions can be rebuilt from their source.
  • This uses a phone camera, not CIRCLE's own hardware, which is designed and not built.
